SMALL OPERANDS, WIDE TOTALS

Why not keep the answer in INT8?

127 × 127 = 16,129

Even one product is too large for INT8. A dot product adds many of them, so our machine uses a checked INT32 running total.

INT8 operands −128 to 127
INT32 totals −2,147,483,648 to 2,147,483,647

Our weight quantizer uses the symmetric subset −127 to 127.

KEEP THE UNITS CONSISTENT

A bias is added in the product’s units.

A learned bias of 0.04 becomes integer 4 at scale 0.01. Rescaling changes the integer to match the next layer’s units. Both final representations mean 0.24.

How to interpret these estimates

These are the cycles for one image’s two matrix stages, including their input, weight and output transfers. Bias, activation, rescaling and digit selection are calculated elsewhere and are not included in the time here. Matrix traffic excludes bias and scale metadata.

The simulator counts an explicit schedule with no zero-skipping. The fixed-dimension sweeps therefore have the same cycle counts as the real-image checks. Equal clock and lane counts do not establish equal physical area, energy or achievable frequency.

A high-level software model of proposed hardware · FP32 and INT8 use the same scheduling rules.
